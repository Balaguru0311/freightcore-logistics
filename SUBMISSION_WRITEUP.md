# Assignment Write-up

I approached FreightCore as an enterprise logistics brand rather than a generic landing-page template. I first translated the brief into three scoring priorities: a memorable scroll sequence, a WebGL element that supports the story, and responsive behaviour that still feels intentional on mobile. I used AI as a pair-programming and review tool: I supplied the brand premise, required section count, animation constraints and performance limits, then iterated on the generated structure. My prompts focused on outcomes such as “keep the 3D scene primitive-based,” “make the service sequence pinned only above the tablet breakpoint,” and “dispose every Three.js resource on unmount.” I reviewed the resulting React, GSAP and Three.js code, adjusted the content hierarchy, and kept the final scope aligned with the two-day assignment.

One important technical decision was to build the hero globe from an icosahedron, points and a torus route instead of loading a detailed truck model. That makes the WebGL canvas feel relevant to a global freight network while avoiding a large model download, texture decoding and extra loading states. The renderer caps device pixel ratio at 1.75, uses basic materials and contains only a few draw calls. This gives the hero motion and depth without making the page dependent on a heavy asset.

The main issue I anticipated was applying a desktop pinned horizontal sequence directly to small screens. That pattern can trap touch scrolling, produce oversized pinned distances and make content difficult to scan. I solved it with GSAP matchMedia: ScrollTrigger creates the pinned horizontal animation only from 900px upward. Below that breakpoint the same service cards become a normal vertical story, with CSS handling the layout. I also added a reduced-motion path that skips scroll animations and stops automatic WebGL rotation while leaving all content visible.

The finished page includes a responsive hero, animated proof points, three service modules, an operational control-tower section, CTA and footer. It uses semantic HTML, lightweight custom visuals and clear cleanup logic, and is ready for deployment and Lighthouse testing.
